# Realistic tongue articulation stays out of reach

No open-source tool, model, or avatar rig exists today that can take speech audio and produce realistic, alveolar/teeth-contact tongue articulation for consonants like L, T, D, N on a VRM avatar in a Babylon.js pipeline — and the honest, well-supported recommendation is to **stay with the current LAM Audio2Expression + ARKit-52 setup and not add a tongue system**, because every path that offers more than today's zero tongue geometry either doesn't exist as usable software, is locked to a proprietary engine the project cannot adopt without abandoning its browser/WebXR architecture, or demands building a custom rig and a hand-authored phoneme rule from scratch. The research field for audio-driven tongue prediction is small, academic, and largely code-incomplete: the single closest hit, Medina et al.'s CVPR 2022 "Speech Driven Tongue Animation," ships an MIT license and a public dataset but its inference code is marked "under construction" and its output format (raw EMA landmarks) isn't rig-ready ([GitHub](https://github.com/salmedina/SpeechDrivenTongueAnimation)). Every mainstream open avatar format — VRoid/VRM, Ready Player Me, MakeHuman, MB-Lab — caps out at ARKit's single `tongueOut` protrusion shape, with zero support for tip position against the alveolar ridge or teeth. The two systems that do model richer tongue shapes, Epic's MetaHuman and NVIDIA's Audio2Face-3D, are both dead ends for this stack specifically: MetaHuman has no credible path to a client-side WebXR scene, and NVIDIA's desktop tooling for driving a custom rig's tongue is unreliable and now deprecated. A cheap, rule-based fallback — detect alveolar phonemes in a forced-aligned transcript and trigger a hand-sculpted tongue morph — is technically buildable in a few days of Blender and scripting work, but no existing open-source project does this end-to-end, and for a VIP student project the realistic payoff (a crude tongue flick synced to four consonant classes, on a mouth most players rarely see closely in VR) does not clearly justify the build time against everything else on a therapy-app roadmap.

## The audio-to-tongue research field is real but unfinished

Academic work on predicting tongue shape from speech exists, but it is thin, scattered across incompatible data modalities, and essentially none of it has shipped as usable, rig-ready software. The standout candidate is **Medina et al.'s "Speech Driven Tongue Animation" (CVPR 2022)**, which trains a self-supervised Wav2Vec audio encoder to predict EMA (electromagnetic articulography) tongue, jaw, and lip landmarks at 50 FPS, with an MIT-licensed GitHub repo and a companion dataset released on Google Drive ([GitHub](https://github.com/salmedina/SpeechDrivenTongueAnimation); [paper](https://salmedina.github.io/papers/Speech_Driven_Tongue_Animation__CVPR_2022.pdf)). The catch: the repo's own README marks its actual inference pipeline as "UNDER CONSTRUCTION," it has only 54 stars and 36 commits, and even if it worked, it outputs raw 3D landmark coordinates rather than blendshapes — a landmark-to-morph-target retargeting layer would need to be built from scratch before it could drive any VRM avatar. Other acoustic-to-articulatory-inversion papers using ultrasound tongue imaging or real-time MRI exist (Porras et al. 2019, Hu et al. 2022, a 2024 rt-MRI paper, and a March 2026 "AURORA" formant-to-tongue model), but none of them surfaced a confirmed public code release in this research pass ([arXiv:1904.06083](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06083); [arXiv:2206.07327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07327.pdf); [arXiv:2411.02037](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02037); [arXiv:2603.17543](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17543)). Hu et al. explicitly flag the structural reason: public ultrasound/EMA tongue corpora are scarce and almost entirely English-only, which is very likely why no "ARKit-52 for tongues" has ever emerged the way it did for jaw and lips — there simply isn't enough public tongue-tracked training data. Separately, real-time tongue visualizers for speech therapy do exist and some are genuinely open-source, most notably **ematoblender** (GPL, Saarland University/DFKI), which streams measured EMA sensor data into Blender ([GitHub](https://github.com/m2ci-msp/ematoblender)) — but these are playback layers for *captured* articulatory sensor data, not predictive audio-to-tongue models, so they cannot be pointed at arbitrary speech audio in a therapy scenario.

## Every open avatar format caps at one tongue shape: protrusion

VRM, Ready Player Me, MakeHuman, and MB-Lab were all checked specifically for tongue-position blendshapes beyond simple out/in motion, and all four converge on the same ceiling: Apple's ARKit-52 standard, whose only tongue entry is `tongueOut`, a single 0–1 protrusion float with no curl, roll, or lateral control ([Unity Live Capture docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/ja/Packages/com.unity.live-capture@4.0/api/Unity.LiveCapture.ARKitFaceCapture.FaceBlendShapePose.html)). **VRoid Studio** does export a tongue mesh, but community "Perfect Sync" tooling only adds more protrusion-family shapes — side-lick, lip-lick, tongue-out variants sold as paid BOOTH.pm kits — aimed at VTuber camera gimmicks, not articulatory accuracy; none target alveolar or teeth contact ([hinzka blog](https://hinzka.hatenablog.com/entry/2021/05/08/105733); [BOOTH item 4229435](https://booth.pm/en/items/4229435)). **Ready Player Me** avatars expose exactly the same single `tongueOut` target, gated behind an explicit `?morphTargets=ARKit` API parameter ([RPM docs](https://docs.readyplayer.me/ready-player-me/api-reference/avatars/morph-targets/apple-arkit)). The VRM specification itself has never adopted a tongue preset at all — its 17 preset expressions cover emotions, visemes, blink, and gaze, and no tongue-blendshape issue was found in the vrm-c/UniVRM or vrm-specification issue trackers, confirming there is no "ARKit-plus-tongue" de facto community standard analogous to Perfect Sync ([vrm-c/vrm-specification](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/0.0/README.ja.md)). Among non-VRM open character tools, **MakeHuman/MPFB2** has a real tongue helper mesh (`helper-tongue`) under a research-friendly CC0/AGPL-style license, and **MB-Lab** has a GPL-licensed, FACS-compatible facial rig with a genuinely morphable tongue in its main mesh ([MPFB docs](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/docs/rigging_mesh_assets.html); [MB-Lab wiki](https://www.github.com/animate1978/MB-Lab/wiki)) — but neither ships a documented alveolar-contact shape; both would require hand-sculpting new morph targets in Blender before use. The pattern is unambiguous: no free, ready-to-use glTF/VRM avatar exists today with alveolar/teeth-contact tongue blendshapes already built in.

## MetaHuman and NVIDIA's richer tongues are architectural dead ends

The two systems that genuinely model tongue position beyond protrusion both fail for reasons specific to this project's Babylon.js/WebXR architecture, not just general difficulty. **Epic's MetaHuman** rig ships 18 named tongue curves — `tongueTipUp`, `tongueTipDown`, `tongueTipLeft`, `tongueTipRight`, `tonguePress`, `tongueRoll`, and more — grounded in FACS AU100, which in principle could represent a tongue tip contacting the alveolar ridge for L/T/D/N ([Epic MetaHuman standards docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/mh-standards-docs/mha_index)). But getting that rig into Babylon.js would mean abandoning the project's static, client-rendered deployment model entirely: Unreal Engine has no native WebXR or browser export, and the only Epic-documented path to a browser — Pixel Streaming — is a server-rendered WebRTC video stream, explicitly marked experimental for VR, with forum-reported bugs like one-eye-black and broken stereo rendering in actual WebXR headset tests ([Epic Pixel Streaming docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/overview-of-pixel-streaming-in-unreal-engine); [forum bug report](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/vr-pixel-streaming-on-ue5-3-1-right-eye-only-1-3-visible/1549347)). No community or official pipeline was found that exports MetaHuman's actual tongue rig into glTF/VRM — the one free glTF exporter for MetaHuman heads carries only the standard ARKit set, i.e. the same single `tongueOut` shape the project already has, meaning even a successful export would gain nothing ([Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/88fa82af-3210-42e1-81f0-9a613da436ef)). **NVIDIA Audio2Face-3D** is the most complete audio-driven tongue pipeline that exists anywhere — it computes an internal 10-dimensional tongue PCA vector and can optionally emit tongue blendshapes via an `enable_tongue_blendshapes` flag ([arXiv:2508.16401](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16401v1); [NVIDIA ACE docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/ace/audio2face-3d-microservice/2.0/text/architecture/audio2face-ms.html)) — but it is a proprietary NVIDIA microservice, not an open standard other tools can target, and NVIDIA's own forum staff describe tongue retargeting to a custom (non-MetaHuman) rig as the most failure-prone part of the system, with one developer reporting "only the tongue tip moved" and NVIDIA staff unable to explain why ([NVIDIA developer forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/transferring-eye-tongue-and-jaw-animations-to-blendshape-model/261269)). Compounding this, NVIDIA deprecated the standalone Audio2Face desktop app and the Omniverse Launcher used to install it as of October 2025, leaving only the microservice/NIM architecture as a supported path going forward ([NVIDIA legacy tools page](https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse/legacy-tools)). Both of the richest tongue systems in existence, in short, are unusable or self-defeating for a browser-based VRM project specifically.

## A cheap rule-based fallback exists on paper, but nobody has built it

Classical, non-ML viseme systems confirm that a lightweight, rule-based tongue trigger is architecturally sound and already proven in shipping commercial software — but no open-source project has assembled the specific pieces this project would need. Meta's **OVRLipSync** 15-viseme set explicitly separates two tongue-bearing consonant classes that map almost exactly onto the project's target sounds: `DD` ("tongue tip touches the ridge behind the upper teeth," covering T/D) and `nn` ("tongue pressed to the ridge," covering N/L) ([VRChat Wiki](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Visemes)). Reallusion's commercial **iClone/Character Creator "8+7 Phoneme Pair"** system goes further, tracking 7 tongue-movement weights independently from 8 lip shapes and defining a viseme literally named **T_L_D_N** that bundles exactly the consonants this project cares about ([Reallusion manual](https://manual.reallusion.com/Character-Creator-4/Content/ENU/4.0/06-Facial-Profile-Editor/8_7_and_1_1.htm)) — proof the rule-based "phoneme class → tongue weight" architecture works in production, even though it is closed-source and FBX/bone-based rather than portable to glTF. Forced aligners like **Gentle** already produce exactly the input such a rule would need: per-phoneme start/end timestamps from audio plus a transcript ([Reanimate docs](https://reanimate.readthedocs.io/en/latest/voice/)). The missing piece is the glue: no existing open-source project combines a forced aligner's phoneme timestamps with a hand-authored tongue-trigger rule end-to-end. The nearest thing, **AnimaSync V1**, is a Rust/WASM phoneme engine that does map 61 phonemes through a 111-dimensional intermediate representation that includes tongue, down to 52 ARKit blendshapes — but its licensing is murky (it advertises itself as both "Open Source" and a "30-day Free Trial"), and it is a packaged engine, not an extractable rule the project could lift piecemeal ([AnimaSync site](https://goodganglabs.github.io/AnimaSync/)). Building this fallback would mean: run Gentle/MFA once per line of dialogue, bucket T/D/N/L phonemes into an alveolar-tongue trigger, sculpt one or two custom morph targets on the existing VRM mesh in Blender, and cross-fade them on top of the current Rhubarb/LAM output — a self-contained, few-day engineering task with no external model dependency, but also one with no existing open-source reference implementation to build from.

## Conclusion

Ranking the realistic options for this specific project: **(1) stay as-is** — zero cost, zero risk, and the honest conclusion given that nothing else surveyed clears the bar of "genuinely better and actually usable" in a Babylon.js/VRM/WebXR stack; **(2)** if tongue motion is judged worth any investment at all, build the minimal rule-based alveolar trigger described above (forced-aligner timestamps + one or two hand-sculpted morph targets on the current or a MakeHuman/MB-Lab-derived mesh) — a bounded, few-day task with no new runtime dependency, the only option that doesn't require either waiting on unfinished research code or abandoning the engine; **(3)** everything else — Medina et al.'s incomplete CVPR 2022 model, MetaHuman, NVIDIA Audio2Face-3D's desktop/retargeting path, DAZ's Genesis 9 tongue morphs — is not worth pursuing, either because the software doesn't actually work yet, because adopting it means rebuilding the whole deployment architecture outside the browser, or because licensing/output format problems remain unresolved even before integration begins. The deeper takeaway is that this isn't a gap this project is uniquely failing to close: the entire open-source and even most of the commercial avatar ecosystem has standardized on ARKit's single `tongueOut` shape, and the handful of systems that go further are either closed research prototypes or locked inside proprietary, non-web game engines — so a VIP student project choosing to skip tongue articulation isn't settling for less than the state of the art offers for free; it's making the same call the entire industry outside of in-house AAA/NVIDIA pipelines has already made.
